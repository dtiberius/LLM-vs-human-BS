A small, transparent case study comparing the epistemic texture of human-written research with LLM output from the same brief.

## Question

Given the same brief, how much of what an LLM writes could be wrong (i.e. is testable), how much of it is anchored to something specific, and how does that compare with a human professional writing the same piece?

## Hypotheses

- H1: LLM output has a lower proportion of testable claims than the human original, per 100 words.
- H2: LLM output has lower lexical concreteness (Brysbaert mean) and lower named-entity and number density.
- H3: LLM hedges are less often anchored (attached to a stated reason, source or quantity) than human hedges, even if the raw hedge rate is similar.
- H4: LLM output contains more unspecified authority ("research shows", "experts agree") that never resolves to a named source.
- H5: Giving the model the human author's sources closes most of the gap on H2 and H4 but less of the gap on H1 and H3.
