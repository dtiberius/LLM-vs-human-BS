Data appendix for brief 05 (CIPD/YouGov survey, 14 January–3 February 2026; total sample 1,342 people professionals and business leaders. Figures reconstructed from the two CIPD articles reporting the survey; interpretation removed.)

Sub-samples: HR professionals 556; HR leaders 97; senior leaders 353.

Confidence in skills to support AI adoption (% agree):
- HR leaders confident in ethical data use: 68%
- HR leaders confident in challenging decisions that could negatively affect the workforce: 67%
- HR professionals confident in learning how to use AI tools: 67%
- HR professionals confident in understanding AI tools' limitations: 64%
- HR leaders confident estimating future workforce needs arising from AI: around one third
- HR leaders confident designing reskilling pathways: 40%
- HR leaders confident leading job redesign: 46%
- Senior leaders: relatively high confidence in identifying opportunities for AI and balancing innovation with risk; lower confidence in understanding legal responsibilities and communicating AI strategy clearly (no figures given).

Reported impact of AI on workers' job performance, by self-assessed capability (% reporting improved job performance):
- HR leaders with higher confidence in these skills: close to 9 in 10
- HR leaders with lower confidence: around half (many reporting little or no impact)
- Same directional pattern among senior leaders and HR practitioners (no figures given).

Stage of AI adoption:
- Around 45% of organisations report AI use is still ad hoc or experimental; only a small minority have fully integrated AI into core operations.
- Around 42% of organisations experimenting with AI fall into the lowest organisational readiness group.
- Among advanced adopters, over 80% report both job redesign and reskilling activity.
- Organisations at ad hoc/experimental stages are less likely to report performance improvements; where AI is integrated into workflows and business processes, reported improvements are much higher, particularly where HR and leadership capability is stronger (no figures given).

Expected impact of AI on headcount in next 12 months:
- Among respondents with lower confidence in the skills: around 7 in 10 expect no impact on headcount.
- Senior leaders expecting no impact: 73% (lower confidence) vs 47% (higher confidence), with a notable increase in those anticipating growth.
- HR leaders show the same direction (no figures given).
