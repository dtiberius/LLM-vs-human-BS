Data appendix for brief 04 (ONS BICS Wave 159, 5–28 June 2026, businesses with 10+ employees unless stated; OPN 6 May–28 June 2026. Interpretation removed.)

Adoption over time (share of businesses using at least one AI technology): Sept–Oct 2023 ~12%; June 2026 ~35%.
By size, June 2026: 0–9 employees 28%; 250+ employees 49%.
Average number of AI technologies per adopting business: ~1.4 (Sept 2023) to ~1.6 (June 2026).
Technology types used, June 2026 (share of businesses): large language models 18%; visual content creation 16%; data processing using machine learning 12%; image processing using machine learning 6%; robotics 2%; other AI 2%.
Extent of use among adopters: 10% report using AI extensively (0–9 employees 17%; 250+ employees 9%). 15% of businesses report more than half of employees use AI in daily work. Larger businesses more often "not sure" on both measures (new questions this wave).
By industry: information and communication 58% (almost three-fifths) use at least one AI technology; private-sector education also high; construction 13%. Higher-adoption industries also report more multiple-technology use.
Employee vs business: 55% of employed/self-employed report using AI for work or education (OPN, GB) vs 35% of businesses (BICS, UK).
Purpose of use: improving business operations ~60% of businesses (slightly lower among smaller businesses; over 60% of larger businesses); providing/personalising products or services second (31% of 0–9 employee businesses); developing new products/services and exploring new markets lower, and more common among smaller businesses.
Adoption method: free-to-use software most common in manufacturing, wholesale and retail, accommodation and food, administrative and support services; purchased external software or ready-to-use services more common in construction, information and communication, professional/scientific/technical.
Headcount impact: around half of businesses report no impact across size bands (highest among 10–249 employees); just under 7% of medium-sized businesses report a decrease; small businesses slightly lower; a notable share "not sure"/"not applicable".
Among businesses using AI to improve operations: 63% no change; 1% increased headcount; 6% decreased headcount (~6% decrease vs smaller share for other purposes).
Roles affected by technology type: visual content creation → creative/design roles, over half of users; image processing → 53% report impacts on administrative/clerical roles and 53% on creative/design roles; ML data processing → administrative/clerical 41%, data analysis 39%.
Barriers (last three months): 41% report no barriers; lack of expertise ~18% among 100–249 employee businesses; cost 7%–14% across size bands; employee concerns, regulation, product availability less frequently reported.
Skills integration: among businesses citing lack of expertise, 62% train/retrain existing staff vs 26% among those reporting no barriers; businesses with no barriers slightly more likely to automate/replace roles. Overall: training/retraining most common (~40% of medium-to-large businesses); ~10% automate or replace roles; recruiting AI-skilled staff ~2% (smallest) to 10% (250+). 11% of businesses report more than half of workforce has received AI-related training.
External comparators quoted: US workers using generative AI for work early 2026, 43%; UK workers ~36% (St. Louis Fed / NBER "Mind the Gap"); executives' surveys: ~78% of US firms and ~71% of UK firms use at least one AI technology ("Firm Data on AI"); DSIT AI Adoption Research 2026: efficiency/productivity most-cited motivation; ONS 2023 management practices article: barriers = identifying use cases, cost, expertise; BoE DMP Jan 2026: most firms report no material AI impact on employee numbers over past three years.
